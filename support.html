GlassHouse · support

A singing wine glass, a Tibetan singing bowl and a glass harp, physically modelled. For iPhone, iPad and the web.

Making the glass sing

  • Circle the rim slowly and steadily, about one lap a second. Real glasses need a moment to catch, and so does this one.
  • The tone comes from friction between finger and glass. If you get a squeak instead, ease off the pressure in the menu (finger), or slow down.
  • Tap inside the glass to flick it with a fingernail.
  • Slide your thumb along the strip at the bottom to pour water in or out. More water, lower pitch: it's the water's mass loading the wall, as in a real glass.

The singing bowl

Circle the outside of the rim slowly with the puja (the leather-wrapped or bare wooden stick), about half a lap a second, and let it build. With bare wood, once the bowl gets loud the stick starts to bounce off the wall and rattles, exactly as a real one does. A bowl's pitch is set mostly by its size (Size, in the menu); water from the strip lowers it only a little.

The glass harp

Four to six glasses, each tuned to a note by the amount of water in it. Play several at once with several fingers, or from the keyboard below them. Tap a glass's note name to retune it or change its shape.

MIDI keyboards

  • Connect a USB MIDI keyboard (with Apple's camera adapter on Lightning devices) and GlassHouse finds it by itself. Its name appears in the menu under MIDI.
  • For a Bluetooth MIDI keyboard, open the menu and choose Bluetooth MIDI, then pick your device.
  • On the glass, a key sets the pitch by pouring exactly enough water. On the harp, each key pours water into whichever free glass can reach its note; switch on Keys rub too and the key also rubs that glass. The mod wheel sets finger pressure.

No sound?

  • Turn the volume up. GlassHouse plays even with the silent switch on.
  • Touch the glass once: audio starts on the first touch.
  • If another app is playing audio, GlassHouse takes over when you start playing.

How it works

Nothing is sampled. The glass is solved as an elastic shell with the finite element method, the water as potential flow inside it, and the finger as stick-slip friction on the rim, all computed live on your device. Open "physics, in detail" in the app for the full story.
